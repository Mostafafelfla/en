Read the following passage.

A. Toronto’s High Park, established in 1876, stands as one of Canada’s most iconic urban green spaces. Donated to the city by John George Howard, a Toronto architect and artist, the park’s creation stemmed from Howard’s vision of preserving natural beauty for public enjoyment. Spanning over 400 acres, High Park balances cultivated areas with untamed landscapes, including gardens, wooded trails, and wetlands. This design has made it a cherished destination for both leisure and exploration, embodying the historical ethos of integrating nature into urban living.

B. Physically, High Park features a diverse array of attractions. The park boasts the Grenadier Pond, an expansive body of water supporting local wildlife, and the rare oak savannah, a remnant of Toronto’s pre-settlement ecosystem. Its manicured gardens, such as the Hillside Gardens, provide a colorful contrast to the rugged trails and forested zones. Recreational amenities, including sports facilities, picnic areas, and a zoo, make it accessible to people of all ages and interests. Such a dynamic landscape underscores High Park's role in fostering community and promoting well-being.

C. Beyond its physical and historical significance, High Park exemplifies the broad benefits of urban green spaces. Its lush greenery contributes to cleaner air, with trees and plants filtering pollutants and cooling the surrounding area. This aligns with broader environmental goals, as parks like High Park mitigate urban heat and reduce city-wide pollution levels. Simultaneously, these spaces enhance the physical and mental health of visitors, offering areas for exercise, relaxation, and connection with nature. In a bustling city like Toronto, High Park serves as a vital refuge for its residents.

D. Economically, the presence of High Park positively impacts nearby real estate, increasing property values for homes in the vicinity. It also attracts tourism, drawing both locals and visitors to its seasonal highlights, such as the famed cherry blossom bloom in spring. The park’s appeal extends to businesses, which benefit from its picturesque surroundings and influx of visitors. In this way, High Park not only enriches Toronto’s environment and culture but also contributes to its economic vitality, showcasing the multifaceted value of urban green spaces.

E. Not given in any of the above paragraphs.
