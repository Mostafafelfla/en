Read the following passage.

A. The Great Pacific Garbage Patch is a massive collection of marine debris located in the North Pacific Ocean. This area is characterized by high concentrations of floating plastics, chemical sludge, and other debris trapped by the currents of the North Pacific Gyre. The patch is not a solid mass but rather a diffuse area where larger pieces of debris, such as discarded fishing nets and plastic bottles, mix with tiny plastic particles known as microplastics. These microplastics, smaller than 5 millimeters, are particularly harmful to marine life as they are easily ingested.

B. The formation of the Great Pacific Garbage Patch is the result of human activities combined with oceanographic processes. Plastic waste that enters the ocean from rivers, coastal areas, and ships is carried by ocean currents. Over time, these currents concentrate the debris in specific areas, forming large patches. The North Pacific Gyre, a system of circular ocean currents, plays a crucial role in trapping and accumulating debris in this region. The movement of the gyre creates a vortex that pulls debris into the center, where it becomes trapped.

C. The environmental impact of the Great Pacific Garbage Patch is profound. Marine animals, such as fish, turtles, and birds, often mistake plastic debris for food, leading to ingestion and potential death. Additionally, the ingestion of microplastics can introduce toxic substances into the food chain, affecting not only marine life but also human health. The debris also damages coral reefs and other marine habitats, disrupting ecosystems and biodiversity. Efforts to clean up the patch are challenging due to its vast size and the constant influx of new debris.

D. Addressing the issue of plastic pollution in the ocean requires global cooperation and multifaceted strategies. Reducing plastic production and consumption is a critical step. Policies that promote recycling and the use of alternative materials can help mitigate the amount of plastic waste entering the ocean. Additionally, cleanup initiatives, both large-scale and community-driven, are essential in removing existing debris. Public awareness campaigns and education can also play a significant role in changing consumer behaviors and encouraging more sustainable practices.

E. Not given in any of the above paragraphs.

Decide which paragraph, A to D, has the information given in each statement below. Select E if the information is not given in any of the paragraphs.

-1. The Great Pacific Garbage Patch contains a mix of large debris and tiny particles.
-2. Human activities and ocean currents contribute to the formation of the garbage patch.
-3. Marine animals are at risk due to ingesting plastic waste found in the patch.
-4. Reducing the production and use of plastic materials is essential to tackle ocean pollution.
-5. The North Pacific Gyre is instrumental in trapping debris within the patch.
-6. The garbage patch poses a threat to marine ecosystems and biodiversity.
-7. Cleanup efforts are complicated due to the continuous addition of new debris.
-8. Public education and awareness campaigns can help change consumer behaviors regarding plastic use.
-9. Plastic waste contributes significantly to ocean acidification.
